Good to know — cash-heavy weeks.

Commission is collected from the card money we hold for you. If a week is mostly cash orders, the card money may not cover commission and fees — the small balance simply carries forward to a future week. Your home page banner shows this whenever it happens.

Lesson 4 · Why the payout can differ View only

Cash, card, refunds & carried balances

A busy week does not always equal the same-size bank deposit. Your statement combines orders paid online with orders paid directly to the restaurant, then applies the charges in your agreement.

Quick answer: Understand a smaller payout

Where: Overview → Statements.

Do this: Open the relevant weekly statement. Compare cash and card sales, deductions, refunds, commission credits, and net payout. Cash collected at your restaurant is not money Menu.ca is holding to pay out.

Check: Match unfamiliar amounts to the orders listed beneath the statement.

The figures still do not make sense

Record the statement period, order number, and amount you are asking about. Send those details through Owner help. Past statements stay frozen; do not manually adjust them.

Cash orders

You already received the money

Cash collected at the restaurant is part of sales, but it is not money Menu.ca is holding to pay out.

Card orders

Card funds move through the statement

Card processing fees apply to card orders. The remaining held funds contribute to the net payout.

Later refunds

Past statements stay frozen

A later refund appears in the statement period when it is processed rather than rewriting an older statement.

Cash-heavy week?

If held card funds do not cover commission and fees, the remaining balance can carry forward. That is not automatically a missing payment.

Before asking about a statement

  • Write down the statement date range.
  • Compare gross sales, deductions, refunds, and the final net payout.
  • Open the order detail beneath the summary for an unfamiliar amount.
  • Use Owner help with the statement period and order number if the figures still do not make sense.
Lesson 5 · Your weekly schedule You can edit

Opening hours

Set the weekly schedule customers order against. One window per day — or add a second for a split shift. Every save goes straight to your live storefront.

Quick answer: Change your regular hours

Where: Hours → Weekly schedule.

Do this: Choose the day, edit the opening and closing times, then save. Use two windows for a lunch-and-dinner split.

Check: Open the customer storefront and check the day and both service windows.

My saved hours look wrong

Confirm the day, 24-hour times, and any dated closure that overrides the weekly schedule. The tablet ordering window is view-only on this page; use Owner help if it is wrong.

Lesson 8 · Same-day emergency You can edit

Stop orders today — then reopen safely

A flood, power outage, equipment failure, or staff shortage needs a same-day action, not a permanent rewrite of your weekly schedule.

Quick answer: Close early today

Where: Hours & availability → Close for the rest of today.

Do this: Apply a same-day closure. Keep your recurring weekly schedule for normal operating hours.

Check: Open the customer storefront and confirm the restaurant is unavailable. Reopen only when you can fulfil new orders.

What about orders already accepted?

Closing today is for stopping new orders. Look up existing orders separately. After acceptance, use Owner help for a cancellation or refund; include the order details and requested outcome.

Right now

Close for the rest of today

Use the same-day closure control when the restaurant cannot take more orders today.

Later today

Reopen when ready

Remove the same-day closure only after the kitchen and ordering setup are genuinely ready.

Tomorrow

Normal hours resume

A same-day closure should not replace your regular weekly schedule.

  • Apply the closure and verify that the customer storefront shows the restaurant unavailable.
  • Check tablets, printers, internet, staffing, and kitchen readiness before reopening.
  • Reopen only when new orders can be accepted and fulfilled.
  • Verify the customer storefront again after reopening.
Do not use this for a vacation or recurring closure.

Use dated exceptions for a planned date and weekly hours for a permanent schedule change.

Lesson 15 · A complete request

Send Owner help everything needed the first time

A clear request protects the wrong order, item, restaurant, or date from being changed. Use Owner help on the portal sign-in page and include identifiers rather than relying on a description alone.

Quick answer: Ask for help

Where: Your restaurant’s portal sign-in page → Owner help.

Do this: Use the template below. Include the exact restaurant, order or item, requested outcome, and effective date.

Check: Keep the team’s confirmation so you know what changed and when.

What should I include?

For an order: number, date, customer, amount, and requested outcome. For a menu change: exact category, dish, wording, sizes, prices, and effective date. Never include passwords, sign-in links, or payment-card details.

Order or refund issue

Restaurant, order number, order date, customer name, amount, and the requested outcome.

Menu change

Restaurant, exact category and item, current wording, requested wording, prices, sizes, and effective date.

Hours or delivery

Restaurant, exact dates or recurring days, opening/closing times, delivery zone or minimum, and when it should start.

Copy this templateRestaurant: Your Restaurant, Your City Request: [exact change or problem] Order/item/category: [exact identifier] Current value or status: [what you see now] Requested value or outcome: [what should change] Effective date: [date and time] Supporting details: [photo or wording, with no payment-card data] Please confirm before changing anything that moves money.
  • Never send a password, sign-in link, full payment-card number, or security code.
  • Double-check the restaurant and identifier before submitting.
  • Use one clear request per issue when several unrelated things need work.
  • Keep the confirmation so you know what was changed and when.
